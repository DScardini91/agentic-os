## TL;DR

**This is for one specific kind of operator:** a senior knowledge worker whose decisions compound, who already uses Claude Code daily, and who wants the discipline of an internal Chief of Staff without hiring one.

**It is not for most people.** That's by design.

---

## ✅ This is for you if

- You are a **senior knowledge worker** — consultant, founder, researcher, strategist, senior IC, executive — whose decisions get re-litigated across weeks and months.
- You already use **Claude Code** (or are willing to install it). This is not a standalone product; it lives on top of Claude Code's harness.
- Your work generates **persistent context** — clients, projects, recurring decisions, frameworks you re-apply. The system's value comes from preserving and compounding that context.
- You are comfortable reading **a few markdown files** before running a setup interview. Roughly 30 minutes of reading + 15 minutes of bootstrap = first use.
- You think of your work in **operator terms** — priorities, decisions, signals, governance, drift — rather than purely in task-list terms.
- You are willing to spend **10 minutes to 1 hour per week** maintaining the system (varies by how deep on the ladder you climb).
- You value **deterministic enforcement** of rules you've set for yourself, not soft reminders the model will drift around under pressure.

If five or more of those describe you, fork it.

---

## ❌ This is NOT for you if

- You want a **chat companion** or a "talk to an AI" experience. This is a workbench, not a relationship.
- You want an **out-of-the-box product**. The whole point is that you instantiate it for yourself. If you don't have time to define what your interface agent should be like, the system has no shape to operate within.
- Your work is **transactional** — high volume, low persistence per task, no compounding context. A ticket-closer, a help-desk responder, a high-frequency support role. The system's overhead exceeds its value for you.
- You operate inside a **regulated environment that prohibits any data residing outside corporate-sanctioned systems**. Today the OS keeps memory on your local machine + your GitHub fork; if either of those is off-limits for your client data, **do not fork until you have read [`DATA_AND_PRIVACY.md`](DATA_AND_PRIVACY.md)** and your IT/compliance has cleared the pattern.
- You expect to **coordinate a team of 8+ people through this system**. It is single-operator by design. Multi-principal is on the roadmap (v3.x territory) but not shipped.
- You are not willing to read documentation. The system has 22 rungs of progressive depth; the depth is real. If 5 minutes of reading frustrates you, this will frustrate you continuously.
- You are looking for **general AI advice**. The system structures *how* Claude operates on *your* work. The work is still yours.

If three or more of those describe you, do not fork. You will install it, hit friction, and bounce.

## 🤔 Maybe-for-you

There's a middle band where the answer is *"it depends"*:

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| You manage **a team of 2–5** | The interface-agent + senior-advisor pattern works well for the single-operator-with-team shape. Beyond ~5 direct reports, multi-principal becomes the limiting factor. |
| You're **early in a career** | The system rewards operators who already have persistent decisions to track. If you haven't yet accumulated decision portfolios that need governance, climb the early career first, then fork. |
| You work in **a highly creative role** (writer, designer, composer) | The system handles craft-as-domain well (see Rung 7 "solo creator" archetype in EVOLUTION_PATH). But if you don't think in operating terms — *priorities, decisions, signals* — the framing will feel alien. |
| You use **ChatGPT Plus + Notion** and you're considering whether to add this | Probably yes, **as a complement, not a replacement**. See [`INTEGRATION_MAP.md`](INTEGRATION_MAP.md) for how the surfaces split. |

## 🎯 What the system optimizes for

If you imagine the operator the system is most useful for, that operator has:

1. **A portfolio of decisions, not a queue of tasks.** Tasks are tracked elsewhere (Notion, Linear, Jira). The system tracks *decisions* — the ones you'd otherwise re-litigate at 11 pm a month later.

2. **Recurring decision shapes.** Engagement acceptance, scope changes, hiring calls, allocation moves. The system shines when the same *kind* of decision shows up regularly enough that frameworks compound.

3. **An external voice that matters.** Clients, board, senior stakeholders, a public audience. The senior-advisor pressure-test before delivery is calibrated for outputs whose downside is reputational, not just operational.

4. **A reading habit with intent.** You read books to extract operating rules, not to entertain. The canon-ingestion pipeline (Rung 8 → 18) compounds for operators who already practice deliberate reading.

5. **Tolerance for opinionated systems.** This is not a neutral tool. It has a topology (single interface + internal advisor + spokes + guardians) and an ethos (deliberate accretion, guide-rail-not-cage, pattern-is-canon-instances-are-personal). If those words register as "yes, of course," you'll feel at home. If they register as "what?", the system will feel alien.

## 🪜 Where the system fits on your career

| Career stage | Fit |
|---|---|
| Early IC / analyst (≤ 3 years) | **Skip.** Build decision portfolios first. Re-evaluate at the next promotion. |
| Senior IC (3–7 years) | **Maybe.** If you've accumulated a recurring client/project portfolio, fork. If still rotating, wait. |
| Lead / manager / senior IC (7–12 years) | **Strong fit.** Most operators who fork this and stay live in this band. |
| Principal / director / partner-track | **Strong fit at higher rungs.** Most active operators settle around Rung 12–17 in this band. |
| C-suite / partner / founder of meaningful scale | **Fit, but the orchestrator pattern (Rung 12+) becomes table stakes.** You'll spend less time at Rungs 1–11 and more time authoring custom canon. |
| Retired / hobbyist | **Fit if intellectual compounding is the goal.** The ladder works for sustained personal-development too — just optimized differently. |

## 🚪 Easy exit

If you fork and within 30 days decide it doesn't fit:

- All your data is in **markdown files** on your local machine + your fork. No vendor lock-in.
- Copy your `control-plane/memory/auto/`, `control-plane/memory/decisions/`, and `control-plane/memory/daily/` into a personal Notion / Obsidian / plain folder. **That's your portable operator history.**
- Delete the fork. The system leaves no residue beyond what you copied out.

You don't lose anything by trying. You lose a weekend if you stay.
