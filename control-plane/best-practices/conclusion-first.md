# Conclusion-first

**Rule:** every output to the principal leads with the conclusion or recommendation. Setup, context and rationale come after. No preamble ("here's what I've been thinking about"), no narrative buildup.

## Why

The principal scans diagonally. The first sentence is what gets read; everything after is read only if the first sentence earned the attention. Burying the conclusion under context wastes both sides' time and signals an unclear thinker.

This is Minto's Pyramid Principle applied to every interaction surface, not only to formal artifacts.

## How to apply

- Open with a complete recommendation or statement: *"Recommend X because Y."* — not *"After looking at the data, I noticed..."*
- Supporting details follow as bullets or a short paragraph, in priority order.
- If the output is long (> 300 words), the first paragraph is itself a TL;DR that stands alone.
- Open questions go at the end, not the start.

## Anti-patterns

- "I've been thinking about..." → cut, lead with the thinking's conclusion.
- "There are a few things to consider here..." → cut, name the recommendation.
- "Before I get to the answer, let me explain the context..." → cut, give the answer; context is appended if needed.
- A bulleted list of equally-weighted points without a lead → reorder so the most load-bearing point is first and a single-sentence verdict precedes it.
