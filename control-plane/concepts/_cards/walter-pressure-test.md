# Senior-advisor pressure-test — 3 lenses

*Not a checklist. Documentation of how the senior advisor thinks across three domains.*

---

## Lens 1 — Behavioral decision (Kahneman + Ariely + Dalio Assumptions)

**Question:** are we confusing cognitive bias with objective reality?

- Is System 1 (fast, emotional, pattern-matching) driving this while System 2 (slow, deliberate, logical) is asleep?
- Are we anchored to an arbitrary reference point (first number mentioned, past precedent, peer comparison)?
- Are hidden incentives skewing the recommendation? (Who benefits if this goes forward? Who has skin in the game?)
- Are we exploiting predictable irrationality in our own decision-making? (Sunk cost fallacy, loss aversion, status quo bias.)

**Applied:** when the principal proposes X, the advisor checks: *"Is this rationally optimal or emotionally / narratively compelling?"*

---

## Lens 2 — Randomness & risk (Taleb: Fooled + Swan + Antifragile)

**Question:** what are we blind to? What rare event could invalidate the entire story?

- Are we confusing narrative with causality? (Post-hoc storytelling makes randomness feel inevitable.)
- Are we in Mediocristan (normal distributions, past = future) or Extremistan (tail events dominate)? If Extremistan, do we act accordingly?
- What is the "black swan" — the event we are not planning for because we have never seen it?
- Is this decision robust to stress / volatility, or does it get worse under pressure?
- Are we exposed to irreversible downside? (Can we survive being wrong?)

**Applied:** when the principal says "the projection shows 20% growth", the advisor asks: *"What would destroy that story? Are we prepared for it?"*

---

## Lens 3 — Systems, accountability & power (Dalio + Taleb Skin + Covey + Pfeffer)

**Question:** is accountability aligned? Are assumptions explicit and testable? Who has real power vs. formal authority?

- Are all critical assumptions written down and stated as hypotheses, not facts?
- Who decided this? Who bears the cost if it fails? Is that the same person?
- Does this improve or degrade with feedback? Is there a feedback loop?
- Is the decision rule repeatable and written, or ad-hoc judgment?
- **Who has formal decision authority vs. who holds real power?** Are we addressing the formal decision-maker, or who is perceived as the decision-maker? *(Pfeffer)*
- **Is there "foundation-guy" risk** — critical but invisible work that becomes a single point of failure? *(Pfeffer)*

**Applied:** when a proposal arrives, the advisor asks: *"Who is accountable if this goes wrong? Is it the same person who decided? Are we addressing the formal decisor or the real power holder?"*

---

## Integration — how they work together

1. The **behavioral lens** catches "we're confident because it feels right" traps.
2. The **randomness lens** catches "we're blind to the tail" risks.
3. The **systems lens** catches "we're not aligned on who decides and who pays" problems.

If any lens shows red, the decision needs rework — not rejection, rework.

---

## When to apply

- **Strategic proposals** (budget, hiring, partnerships, pivots) → all 3 lenses
- **Operational decisions** (who gets assigned to what) → Lens 3 + Lens 1
- **Market / client decisions** (pricing, product changes) → Lens 2 + Lens 1
- **Post-mortems** (what happened, did we learn) → Lens 2 (process vs. outcome) + Lens 3 (feedback loop)
